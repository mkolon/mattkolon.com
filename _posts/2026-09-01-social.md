title: Why I'm not on Social Media
---

What I want to say is too important to me to be posted there

***

I dislike the very idea of social media. I admit that in the beginning, I bought in to the idea that my 15 minutes of fame would be brought about by my timely and witty bon mots, and I played in that world for a bit. But when Facebook began to attract so much spite and garbage, I closed my account. That was in 2012. 
I have a Twitter account which I use to monitor a few things and people dear to my heart, but I almost never post. I am of two minds: That no one is much interested in my blather, and that my blather is worth more than a fleeting post rapidly dropping beneath the event horizon. Immortality is asking a lot, but more than trivial engagement shouldn't be that hard, should it?
Most of all, though, I'm horrified by the idea that my  algorithmically-exploited digital twin will represent me to the rest of the world. This site is mine, and while it's free for anyone to read, I can control what it says about me. I don't suffer from a need to be seen. I have been in positions and situations like that before, and I prefer to manage them more closely than the viral world allows.
I'm worried about the extent to which we all watch each other now, and while I'm probably not as concerned about government survellience as I should be, I have always found the words of Keith Lowell Jensen precient: “What Orwell failed to predict is that we'd buy the cameras ourselves, and that our biggest fear would be that nobody was watching.” 
This doesn't seem healthy to me. It requires a preoccupation with being performative, always thinking of what your next performance will be.
