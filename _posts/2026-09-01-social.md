What I want to say is too important to me to be posted there

***

I dislike the very idea of social media. I admit that in the beginning, I thought that maybe my 15 minutes of fame would be brought about by my timely and witty *bons mots,* and I played in that world for a bit. But when Facebook began to attract so much spite and garbage, I closed my account. That was in 2012. 
I have a Twitter account which I use to monitor a few things and people dear to my heart, but I almost never post. I am of two minds: That no one is much interested in my trivial blather, and that my less-trivial blather is worth more than a fleeting post, rapidly dropping beneath the event horizon. Immortality is asking a lot, but more than trivial engagement shouldn't be that hard, should it?
Most of all, though, I'm horrified by the idea that my  algorithmically-exploited digital twin will represent me to the rest of the world without my consent or knowledge. Mattkolon.com is mine, and while it's free for anyone to read, I can control what it says about me. I don't suffer from a need to be seen. I have been in positions and situations like that before, and I prefer to manage them more closely than the viral world allows.
I'm worried about the extent to which we all watch each other now, and while I'm probably not as concerned about government survellience as I should be, I have always found the words of Keith Lowell Jensen precient: “What Orwell failed to predict is that we'd buy the cameras ourselves, and that our biggest fear would be that nobody was watching.” 
This doesn't seem healthy to me. It requires a preoccupation with being performative, of always thinking of what your next act will be.
Furthermore, the whole game is about working your needle to the top of the haystack, and I find it a bit exhausting. If you're already famous, the world is waiting to hear from you. But for most of us, we're actually posting to our core of friends and letting acquaintences see, too. We do this in hopes that this extended group links to an influencer who will boost our post (and maybe us) into the stratosphere, where the real money is.
